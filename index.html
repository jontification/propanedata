Stock-change reconciliation flags

Adjacent observations exactly seven days apart where the change in national stock levels differs from the reported build/draw by more than 0.2 million barrels. This screen does not identify the cause and is not an exhaustive historical audit.

Methodology and guardrails

One observation, many displays

Cards, charts, tables and numerical commentary are generated from the same embedded data object. There is no separate set of hand-updated signal values and no remote feed changing prices underneath a dated narrative.

Calendar time, not array position

Seasons run April–March. The first scheduled Friday in April is week 1. Non-Friday records are mapped to that calendar week for chart alignment while the original date and source row remain in the exported data. Earlier gaps are preserved in the full archive; they are not compressed into artificial consecutive weeks.

Two legitimate build measures

Build-pace charts sum the workbook’s reported weekly builds. Stock bridges and historical replay paths use differences between inventory levels. Both are retained because the historical workbook does not always reconcile. A build sum is not silently substituted for an observed stock level.

No look-ahead claim

These are descriptive charts and conditional replays, not trading backtests. The full point-in-time price history, release timestamps, transaction costs and forward contract quotes needed to test a buying rule are not supplied.
